Retail data science, end to end

Five personal projects on a simulated grocery retailer: 120 products, 104 weeks and 6,000 loyalty customers. The data is synthetic and none of this is client work. Because the simulator knows the true elasticities, churn and transfer rates, every method is checked against ground truth as well as a holdout. All code is on GitHub and reproduces these numbers exactly.

Promotions

Promotion uplift decomposition

A promotion sold 300 extra units. How many were genuinely new to the business?

Method
Per-product log-linear demand model with seasonality, price, promo, display, post-promo dip and cannibalisation terms. Each of 1,488 promotions is split into gross lift, cannibalised, pulled-forward and net units.
Result
25% of gross promo volume isn't incremental. 82% of promotions lose margin, and every depth beyond 10% off is margin-negative on average.

Pricing

Elasticity shrinkage and price optimisation

Where can shelf prices move to grow margin without hurting revenue or price perception?

Method
Per-product elasticities from regular-price weeks, shrunk toward category means with empirical Bayes. SLSQP then optimises within ±10% price moves, a revenue floor and a price-index cap, and the plan is scored against true demand.
Result
Elasticity error falls from RMSE 5.96 to 0.37. The plan earns +4.8% margin, 92% of what perfect knowledge would deliver.

Customer value

Customer lifetime value

Who will be worth the most over the next six months, and what will the whole base spend?

Method
BG/NBD for purchase rate and silent churn, plus Gamma-Gamma for spend, both fitted by maximum likelihood from scratch. Trained on 78 weeks and tested on the next 26.
Result
Aggregate spend forecast within 2.2%, against +182% for a run-rate baseline. The top decile captures 62% of future spend.

Customer understanding

Need-state segmentation with LDA

What missions do customers shop for, and how does each customer mix them?

Method
Latent Dirichlet Allocation over customer × subcategory purchases, compared with K-means and checked against the true need states.
Result
Recovers five need states with 89% accuracy, and each customer's mission mix at r = 0.90, which hard clustering can't give. 54% of customers have a meaningful second mission.

Assortment

Demand transferability for range reviews

If 15% of SKUs go, which can be delisted without losing the sales?

Method
Out-of-stock weeks used as natural experiments to measure how much demand moves to sibling products, with shrinkage toward subcategory rates. Delist choices are scored with true transfer.
Result
A transfer-aware delist loses 19% less revenue than cutting the lowest sellers, closing 57% of the gap to the oracle.
