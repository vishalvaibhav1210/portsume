L&D training bot

How do you get thousands of staff onto the right live training without anyone opening the LMS?

An always-on, rolling 30-day training calendar in chat, with register, enrol and unenrol. A shared cache cut API calls by 65% and kept "system busy" errors at zero at peak.

Case study

Problem

Live sessions were buried in the LMS. People missed training, and admins spent their days booking seats by hand.

My role

Designed the conversation, built the bot and the integration, and ran the rollout with the L&D team.

Approach

Intents for browse, certifications, register, cancel and my bookings, all from inside Teams. One shared, always-trimmed 30-day calendar is cached with stale-while-revalidate.

Validation

Four automated tests, plus a replay of 400 employees sending 1,200 messages around two reminder emails, with and without the cache.

Result

API calls fell from 1,192 to 416. "System busy" replies fell from 317 to 0, and more bookings went through.

What's next

Waitlists for full sessions, manager approvals, and LLM-based intent handling for free-text requests.

Promotion uplift decomposition

A promotion sold 300 extra units. How many were genuinely new to the business?

25% of gross promo volume isn't incremental. 82% of 1,488 promotions lose margin, and every depth beyond 10% off is negative on average.

Code ↗
Case study

Problem

Promotion plans are judged on gross uplift, which counts sales taken from sister products and from next week.

Data

120 products × 104 weeks with price, promo depth, display, out-of-stocks and seasonality.

Approach

A log-linear demand model per product with promo, display, post-promo dip and subcategory cannibalisation terms. Each event is split into gross, cannibalised, pulled-forward and net units, and margin also charges the subsidy on baseline units.

Validation

Holdout WAPE of 13.3%, against 30.6% for a naive mean. Recovered parameters correlate with the truth: cannibalisation r = 0.91, elasticity r = 0.78.

Business read

Shallow discounts with display support do the work. Deep cuts mostly buy volume the business already had.

Limits

Halo effects across categories aren't modelled yet. A hierarchical model would stabilise small products.

Elasticity shrinkage & price optimisation

Where can shelf prices move to grow margin without hurting revenue or price perception?

Empirical-Bayes shrinkage cuts elasticity error from RMSE 5.96 to 0.37. The optimised plan earns +4.8% margin, 92% of the oracle.

Play with elasticity ↓Code ↗
Case study

Problem

Each product changes shelf price only a few times, so per-product elasticities are too noisy to price from.

Approach

OLS on clean regular-price weeks, then shrinkage toward the category mean weighted by each standard error, with a robust (MAD) between-product variance.

Optimisation

SLSQP per category: maximise margin with ±10% moves, revenue at least 99% of today and a price index of at most 1.02.

Validation

Every plan is scored on the simulator's true demand curves, not the model's own estimates.

Result

Raw estimates earn +3.9%, shrunk +4.8% and the oracle +5.3%.

What's next

Cross-price effects inside the optimiser, and a full hierarchical Bayesian fit.

Customer lifetime value

Who will be worth the most over the next six months, and what will the base spend?

BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma written from scratch. Aggregate spend comes within 2.2%, against +182% for run-rate, and the top decile captures 62% of future spend.

Code ↗
Case study

Problem

Retention and CRM budgets need a forward-looking value per customer, not past spend.

Approach

BG/NBD for purchase rate and silent churn, and Gamma-Gamma for spend per trip. Both log-likelihoods are maximised directly in SciPy.

Validation

78-week calibration and 26-week holdout. The holdout trip count is within 1.1% in aggregate.

Result

Spearman 0.71 against 0.41 for run-rate, which ignores churn and nearly triples the forecast.

Need-state segmentation with LDA

What missions do customers shop for, and how does each customer mix them?

Five need states with 89% accuracy, and each customer's mission mix at r = 0.90, which K-means can't give. 54% of customers have a real second mission.

Case study

Problem

Hard segments put each shopper in one box, which hides the cross-sell signal.

Approach

LDA on customer × subcategory counts, with topics named by Hungarian matching. K-means on shares is the baseline.

Validation

On dominant-state labels the two are level (ARI 0.74 each). LDA also recovers the mixes, with a mean absolute error of 0.076.

Business read

Target a shopper's second mission, for example Convenience shoppers who also stock up for the family.

Demand transfer for range reviews

If 15% of SKUs go, which can be delisted without losing the sales?

Using out-of-stocks as natural experiments, a transfer-aware delist loses 19% less revenue than cutting the lowest sellers.

Code ↗
Case study

Problem

"Cut the tail" removes products whose shoppers have nowhere else to go.

Approach

In 254 out-of-stock weeks, compare sibling over-performance with the missing product's expected sales. Rates are shrunk toward the subcategory.

Result

Revenue lost falls from 2.17% to 1.76%, against 1.45% for the oracle, which closes 57% of the gap.

Limits

Chain-level data is noisy per product. Customer-level switching from loyalty data is the next step.
